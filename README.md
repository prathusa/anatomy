# Anatomy · Anatomie

A free, interactive anatomy study tool in **English, German and Latin**.
It is built for people who learn anatomy by tracing the **connections**: which nerve supplies which muscle or organ, which segments it carries, which artery feeds it, and what is lost when a nerve is cut.

The site is built **region by region**. The **upper limb**, the **lower limb**, the **back and trunk wall**, the **thorax**, the **abdomen**, the **pelvis and perineum** and the **head and neck** are complete; neuroanatomy follows next (see [Roadmap](#roadmap)). Switch regions with the menu at the top.

*Kostenloses, interaktives Lernwerkzeug für Anatomie auf Deutsch, Englisch und Latein. Es zeigt, wie Nerven, Muskeln, Organe, Knochen, Arterien und Venen zusammenhängen und was bei einer Nervenläsion ausfällt. Die Seite wächst Region für Region; obere und untere Extremität, Rücken und Rumpfwand, Thorax, Bauchsitus, Becken und Damm sowie Kopf und Hals sind fertig.*

## Features

- **3D view**: a schematic model of each region. Rotate, zoom, hover for names, click to select, and toggle each layer on or off.
  - Upper limb: 8 bones, 49 muscles or muscle groups, 28 nerves (brachial plexus roots to terminal branches) and 20 arteries.
  - Lower limb: 9 bones, 39 muscles or muscle groups, 31 nerves (lumbosacral plexus roots to terminal branches) and 17 arteries.
  - Back and trunk wall: 6 bones (vertebral column, ribs, sternum, occipital bone), 24 muscles or muscle groups (intrinsic back muscles, suboccipital muscles, thoracic wall, diaphragm, abdominal wall), 18 nerves (spinal nerves, dorsal rami and their branches, intercostal, subcostal and phrenic nerves) and 15 arteries.
  - Thorax: 15 organs (heart with its chambers, valves and conducting system; pericardium, lungs, pleura, trachea, main bronchi, oesophagus, thymus), 16 arteries (aorta and its arch branches, coronary and pulmonary arteries, bronchial and oesophageal branches), 9 veins (brachiocephalic veins, venae cavae, azygos system, pulmonary veins, coronary sinus) and 9 autonomic nerves (vagus and recurrent laryngeal nerves, sympathetic trunk, splanchnic nerves, cardiac, pulmonary and oesophageal plexuses).
  - Abdomen: 23 organs (stomach, duodenum, jejunum, ileum, caecum, appendix, the four parts of the colon, liver with a Couinaud segments card, gallbladder, bile ducts, pancreas, spleen, kidneys, ureters, suprarenal glands; peritoneum, greater and lesser omentum, mesentery), 26 arteries (coeliac trunk and its branches, superior and inferior mesenteric arteries and their branches, marginal artery, renal, suprarenal and gonadal arteries), 12 veins (abdominal IVC, hepatic, renal and gonadal veins; the portal vein with splenic, superior and inferior mesenteric and left gastric veins; the oesophageal, rectal and paraumbilical portocaval anastomoses) and 8 autonomic nerves (anterior and posterior vagal trunks, least and lumbar splanchnic nerves, coeliac, superior mesenteric, aorticorenal/renal and inferior mesenteric ganglia and plexuses).
  - Pelvis and perineum: the hip bone, 10 muscles (pelvic floor: puborectalis, pubococcygeus, iliococcygeus, coccygeus; perineum: external anal and urethral sphincters, bulbospongiosus, ischiocavernosus, superficial and deep transverse perineal muscles), 16 organs (bladder, pelvic ureter, male and female urethra, rectum, anal canal; uterus, uterine tubes, ovaries, vagina, clitoris; prostate, seminal vesicles, ductus deferens, testis, penis), 14 arteries (anterior and posterior divisions of the internal iliac artery and their branches: umbilical, vesical, uterine, vaginal, middle rectal, internal pudendal, inferior rectal and perineal arteries), 8 veins (common and internal iliac veins, vesical and uterine venous plexuses, rectal veins and the rectal venous plexus) and 12 nerves (S4 and S5 roots, pudendal nerve and its branches, nerve to levator ani, coccygeal plexus, pelvic splanchnic and hypogastric nerves, inferior hypogastric plexus and cavernous nerves).
  - Head and neck: 10 bones (frontal, parietal, temporal, sphenoid, ethmoid, nasal, maxilla, zygomatic, mandible, hyoid), 46 muscles or muscle groups (facial expression, mastication, suprahyoid and infrahyoid, sternocleidomastoid and scalenes, tongue, pharynx and soft palate, larynx, extraocular muscles and the superior tarsal muscle), 9 organs (thyroid and parathyroid glands, larynx, pharynx, tongue, parotid, submandibular and lacrimal glands, eye), 13 arteries (external and internal carotid, the external carotid branches, ophthalmic and middle meningeal arteries, thyrocervical trunk), 6 veins (internal and external jugular, subclavian, facial and thyroid veins, the dural venous sinuses as one card) and 49 nerves (cranial nerves I–XII; V1, V2, V3 and the branches of the mandibular nerve; the facial nerve's intratemporal branches and its five parotid branches; the corticonuclear tract to the facial nucleus; glossopharyngeal and vagus branches including the pharyngeal plexus and the superior, internal, external and inferior laryngeal nerves; the cervical plexus with the ansa cervicalis and its cutaneous branches; the cervical sympathetic trunk).
  - Neighbouring structures from other regions are shown for orientation: e.g. the spine and ribs in the upper limb, the hip bone in the back, and the chest wall, diaphragm, phrenic nerve, thoracic aorta and subclavian arteries in the thorax, and the abdominal wall muscles, lumbar vertebrae, ribs, hip bone, abdominal aorta, vagus and splanchnic nerves in the abdomen, and the sacrum, iliac vessels, sacral roots, ureters and sigmoid colon in the pelvis, and the cervical vertebrae, ribs, sternum, clavicle, trapezius, vagus, recurrent laryngeal and phrenic nerves, common carotid and vertebral arteries in the head and neck.
- **Explore**: each structure has a card. All names are shown in DE, EN and Latin.
  - Muscles: origin, insertion and action, innervation with spinal segments, arterial supply and a clinical note.
  - Organs: function, parts, sympathetic, parasympathetic and sensory innervation with segments, blood supply, venous drainage and a clinical note.
  - Veins: what they drain into and which veins they receive.
- **Connection graph**: the graph has three views:
  - the selected structure's neighbourhood;
  - the region's whole plexus (brachial, lumbosacral, the spinal nerves and their rami, the autonomic nerves of the thorax and abdomen, the pelvic nerves, or the cranial nerves);
  - the plexus down to its muscles or organs, with edges coloured by sympathetic, parasympathetic and sensory fibres.
  An enlarge button (⤢) gives a full-screen view.
- **Lesion simulator**: cut any root, part of the plexus or nerve. Muscles turn red if paralysed and orange if weakened, each with the segments it lost. The rule is that only segments carried by the cut nerve are lost: a muscle is paralysed when every innervation it has is lost, and weakened when only some are. Organs work the same way per kind of fibre: an organ is denervated when all its innervation is lost and partly denervated otherwise, and the card names which kind (sympathetic, parasympathetic, sensory) is lost or reduced. A clinical picture is included, e.g. wrist drop, claw hand, Erb and Klumpke palsy, foot drop, Trendelenburg sign, phrenic nerve palsy, herpes zoster, recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy, Horner syndrome, coeliac plexus block, vagotomy, referred pain from the appendix (periumbilical, then McBurney point) and the gallbladder (right shoulder via the phrenic nerve), pudendal nerve lesion with loss of the external sphincters, pudendal block, pelvic splanchnic injury with bladder and erectile dysfunction, peripheral vs central facial palsy (the forehead is spared in a central lesion because the upper face is supplied from both hemispheres), oculomotor palsy ("down and out" with ptosis and a dilated pupil), abducens and trochlear palsy, hypoglossal palsy (the tongue deviates towards the side of the lesion), accessory nerve injury after neck surgery, and recurrent and external laryngeal nerve injury after thyroidectomy. Vessel and organ cards cover portal hypertension and the portocaval anastomoses, the splenic flexure watershed, the ureter passing under the uterine artery, haemorrhoids, and pelvic floor injury at birth.
- **Quiz**: six question types, drawn from the current region (only the types that fit the region are offered):
  - name the highlighted structure;
  - which nerve supplies a muscle;
  - which muscle is paralysed after a given lesion;
  - DE ↔ EN translation;
  - which muscle performs a given action;
  - which artery or nerve supplies an organ.
- **Links**: URLs can be shared and bookmarked, e.g. `#explore/n_ulnar`, `#lesion/n_common_fibular`, `#quiz/lower_limb`, `#lesion/n_vagus`, `#lesion/n_pudendal`, `#lesion/n_corticonuclear_facial`.

## Run locally

No build step and no dependencies to install: it is a static site.

```bash
python3 test/serve.py 8000      # then open http://127.0.0.1:8000
node test/validate.mjs          # data integrity + lesion-model + quiz checks
```

Any static web server works; `test/serve.py` additionally disables caching for development.
To publish it, copy the folder to any static host, e.g. GitHub Pages or Netlify.

### Adding a region

1. Create `js/regions/<region>.js` exporting `structures`, `muscleGroups` and `meta` (start structure, plexus name, nerve list groups, home camera), in the same format as `lower-limb.js`.
   - `meta.context` (optional) lists structures from other regions to show for orientation; `meta.contextOpacity` fades them.
   - A nerve or artery made of several separate segmental branches can use `paths` (a list of polylines) instead of `path`.
   - A structure may name a parent or innervating nerve from another region (e.g. the abdominal wall muscles name the iliohypogastric nerve); a muscle's first nerve must be in its own region.
   - Organs (`type: 'organ'`, see `thorax.js`) list `nerves` with a `kind` (sympathetic, parasympathetic, sensory), `arteries`, `veins`, an optional `partOf`, and a `shape` made of ellipsoids, spheres, tubes and rings. Their list groups come from `meta.organGroups`; `meta.plexusTargets` renames the "plexus → muscles" graph view.
   - Veins (`type: 'vein'`) name the vein or organ they drain into as `parents`.
2. Import it in `js/data.js` and mark the region `available` in `BODY_REGIONS`.
3. Add its lesion cases to `test/validate.mjs` and run the checks.

## Accuracy and sources

Content follows standard textbook teaching, chiefly Moore's *Clinically Oriented Anatomy* and the *Prometheus LernAtlas*. German names follow common usage in German lectures.
Segmental innervation varies by about one segment between sources and between individuals. Check against the atlas your course uses.
The 3D model is **schematic**: positions and courses are simplified to show relationships, not exact morphology.

**This is a learning aid only and is not for clinical decisions.** Corrections are welcome: please open an issue that names the structure and cites a source.
